## 3. Adversarial simplification result

The core got smaller during replay.

Removed after replay:

- separate repair-action list — duplicated each delta unit's `after` state;
- generic open-question list — duplicated evidence/result/adjudication notes;
- trigger timestamp — unused in the bounded job;
- Needle class metadata;
- full source graph;
- full rubric duplication.

One distinction was added because the negative control required it:

> **owner-adopted change != accepted repair**

Therefore the core separately records:

- `change_status`;
- `repair_status`.

This prevents a real repository revision from being mislabeled as a validated correction
merely because it exists.

## 4. Strongest red team — this may be ordinary issue-template hygiene

This objection remains plausible.

DELTA dispute issues already record:

- criterion;
- problem;
- legal authority;
- grading consequence;
- suggested repair.

Harvey maintenance issues can record:

- task scope;
- reproducible inconsistency;
- score consequence;
- proposed fix;
- exact revision context.

Legal Benchmarks also publicly describes legal review, human escalation and versioned
evaluation cycles.

Therefore:

> **Needle has not established a unique maintenance concept.**

The residual claim is narrower:

> making **prior-result comparability / rejudge / rerun consequence** a required first-class
> output, tied to the specific evidence/time-owned score-bearing delta, might reduce
> reopening/reasoning burden.

That is an externally testable workflow claim.

If a real evaluator already has an equivalent artifact/process, Candidate A should lose
quickly rather than invent a distinction.

## 5. Strongest economic red team — the core may optimize the cheap part

The delta card begins **after a maintenance trigger exists**.

But in real work, the expensive part may be:

- discovering that law/source state changed;
- understanding the new rule;
- deciding the legally correct answer;
- resolving contested professional judgment.

The card does not automate those tasks.

It mainly structures:

- propagation;
- evidence/time ownership;
- affected criteria;
- repair state;
- historical-result consequence.

Therefore an external pilot must measure **full end-to-end qualified effort**, not only time
spent filling or reviewing the card.

If legal detection/adjudication dominates cost and the delta layer saves little, Candidate A
does not have enough added value.

This is a primary kill path.

---

## 6. Strongest operational red team — structured record overhead

A JSON schema can easily become process theater.

External users should **not** be required to author JSON, learn Needle taxonomy or operate a
new UI.

Morrow may maintain the structured record internally.

The user-facing intervention is the compact card.

If keeping the structured record costs more than it prevents in future reopening, remove it.

The schema is an internal reproducibility tool, not the product.
