Standard summary

What these rules do

The EU medical-device framework sets rules for placing, supplying and putting medical devices and in-vitro diagnostic devices into service in the Union. It covers evidence and conformity before market entry, responsibilities in the supply chain, traceability, and monitoring after devices reach the market.

The framework is built around two core acts: the MDR for medical devices and the IVDR for in-vitro diagnostic devices. Patients, users and healthcare professionals are affected by the safety and performance system, while economic operators such as manufacturers, authorised representatives, importers and distributors carry specific regulatory responsibilities.

Relevance

Who and what is covered?

Start with the product, the activity and the role. These are the main routes into the framework; they are not a personal legal determination and the role list is not exhaustive.

MDR

Medical Devices Regulation

Regulation (EU) 2017/745

Medical devices

Start here for medical devices for human use and accessories. The MDR also reaches specified Annex XVI product groups without an intended medical purpose when the Regulation's conditions apply.

Boundary: the MDR does not cover IVDs governed by the IVDR, and it contains other product-boundary and exclusion rules that may matter.

IVDR

In Vitro Diagnostic Medical Devices Regulation

Regulation (EU) 2017/746

Diagnostic devices

Start here for in-vitro diagnostic medical devices for human use, their accessories and performance studies. The definition depends on the manufacturer's intended purpose for examining human specimens and providing diagnostic or related information.

Important laboratory boundary: general laboratory or research-use products are outside the IVDR unless, considering their characteristics, the manufacturer specifically intends them for in-vitro diagnostic examination.

Main regulated roles

You do not have to be the manufacturer to need these rules

Commission economic-operator guide
  • ManufacturerDesigns/manufactures or has the device made and markets it under its name; carries the primary conformity system.
  • Authorised representativeActs in the Union under a written mandate for specified tasks for a manufacturer established outside the Union.
  • ImporterPlaces a device from a third country on the Union market and has its own verification, traceability and cooperation duties.
  • DistributorMakes devices available in the supply chain and has specified checks and duties around non-conformity, complaints and cooperation.
Do not infer exclusion from this list. Other roles and special settings can matter, roles can overlap, and duties depend on the product and activity. This page routes the legal questions; it does not decide an individual compliance position.

Landmark dates and current operation

Two core application dates — plus a current EUDAMED milestone

26 May 2021

MDR general application / predecessor replacement

Regulation 2017/745 replaced Directives 90/385/EEC and 93/42/EEC from this date, subject to transition rules.

26 May 2022

IVDR general application / predecessor replacement

Regulation 2017/746 replaced Directive 98/79/EC from this date, subject to transition rules.

These dates do not settle every legacy-device case. Later EU measures preserve or extend transition routes for specified devices subject to conditions. The relevant product, certificate/declaration, risk class and timing can change the answer. Commission transition overview IVDR transition detail
Current operational milestone — 28 May 2026. The first four EUDAMED modules became mandatory to use from this date after Commission Decision (EU) 2025/2371 declared them functional and the six-month transition triggered by Regulation (EU) 2024/1860 ran. This does not mean all six EUDAMED modules are already mandatory. Commission EUDAMED overview Decision (EU) 2025/2371

Practical effect

What the rules require

Responsibilities differ by role and device. These are orientation points, not a substitute for the relevant provisions.

Manufacturers

Ensure devices are designed and manufactured to the applicable requirements, maintain risk/evaluation systems and technical documentation, and support post-market monitoring.

MDR Article 10 IVDR Article 10

Importers

Carry their own checks before placing third-country devices on the Union market and have traceability, cooperation and corrective responsibilities.

Commission role guidance

Distributors

Must act with due care and perform specified checks before making devices available, with duties around complaints, non-conformity and cooperation.

Commission role guidance

Other affected groups

Patients, users, healthcare professionals, health institutions, notified bodies and authorities can all be affected by the framework, but their position is not the same as an economic operator's duties.
