## Test question

> Does the Low Voltage Directive Navigator help readers answer known-act orientation and verification questions more accurately or with materially less navigation effort than a competent concise source-linked note built from the same evidence?

This is a formative comparison, not a market study or legal validation exercise.

## Participants

Initial formative target: **6**.

Extend to 8 only when the first six expose materially different reader patterns that two additional sessions could reasonably resolve.

Useful mix:
- occasional EU-law readers with a concrete study/work reason;
- legal/regulatory/compliance professionals or near-professionals.

Low Voltage Directive expertise is not required.

Do not infer population-level findings from this sample.

## Session format

Target: **20–25 minutes**.

1. 2–3 min — orientation, consent and boundaries.
2. 7 min — representation 1 + task set.
3. 7 min — representation 2 + disjoint comparable task set.
4. 5–8 min — verification and debrief.

Do not teach the content during the task blocks.

Participants may open official sources when they choose. Record when and why.

## Facilitator opening

Use substantively this wording:

> You are testing two ways of getting oriented around one EU directive. Neither is legal advice and neither replaces the official legal sources. I am testing the material, not you. Please answer from what you can find in the representation in front of you. You may open official source links whenever you want. I may ask what led you to an answer, but I will not tell you whether it is correct until the task block is finished.

Do not describe the Navigator as innovative, superior or comprehensive.

## Timing

Start timing when the task is read/shown.

Stop when the participant gives a final answer or says they cannot answer.

Record:
- completion time;
- whether they opened a source;
- approximate navigation/search actions;
- confidence;
- answer verbatim enough to assess the material inference.

Do not optimise the session around a composite speed score.

## Consequential false-inference stop rule

Stop the session series and return to product repair if the Navigator itself induces any of these:

- voltage-range match = automatic applicability/full legal coverage;
- “Member States” = only entities practically concerned;
- “in force” = one simple application date;
- a framework/context act = an ancestor;
- importing one external provision = importing the whole external act;
- Article 12 presumption = complete product compliance;
- frozen/verified evidence = live legal monitoring.

One participant misunderstanding can be user error; a UI-induced inference is a product defect. Record the evidence before deciding which occurred.

## Comparative decision after six

Do not rank participants or generate an opaque total score.

Review by task family:

- correctness / consequential error;
- time and navigation effort;
- evidence/source finding;
- confidence vs correctness;
- preferred starting representation;
- reasons for preference.

Then apply:

- if Navigator provides no material reduction in navigation/interpretive effort versus the strong note, **NARROW or PARK** rather than add rescue features;
- if value is concentrated among occasional readers, narrow the audience claim;
- if professionals primarily use it as orientation before EUR-Lex, that is a valid narrower job;
- if evidence is too LVD-specific, state that before any second-act experiment.
