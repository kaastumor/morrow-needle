# Issue #86 — Needle Method review

The Method stage changes **zero** of the five baseline conclusions.

That is not a failure.

It tells us exactly what kind of contribution the method might have.

## What Method adds

It turns several good-research instincts into explicit, reusable checks:

- presumption ≠ final legal status;
- source origin ≠ legal authority;
- one judicial target ≠ every related legal mechanism;
- scope must travel with its date;
- current state ≠ historical source state;
- language-specific correction ≠ universal correction;
- uncertainty about one surface does not erase direct evidence from another.

## What Method does not add

It did not rescue a wrong answer.

The strong baseline already behaved carefully.

That means we may not claim:

> Needle Method is needed to answer these five questions correctly.

The strongest case for Method is the Dutch corrigendum question. Explicitly
asking for:

- temporal perspective;
- authentic language expression;
- correction event;
- current-vs-historical ownership;

makes the investigation more reproducible and less dependent on the researcher
remembering the right caution spontaneously.

The weakest case is the common-charger control. Method adds essentially nothing
beyond competent reading.

## Interim identity result

**Needle Method survives as a contribution candidate.**

But its value hypothesis has changed.

It is not:

> a method that can solve cases the strong baseline cannot solve.

The plausible claim is now:

> a method that makes high-risk legal-information distinctions systematic,
> reviewable and repeatable across researchers/sessions.

That claim still needs evidence.

Persistent software has not yet earned itself in this gate.
