EU medical-device rules

A source-linked orientation to the two core EU Regulations for medical devices and in-vitro diagnostic medical devices.

What these rules do

The EU medical-device framework sets rules for placing, supplying and putting medical devices and in-vitro diagnostic devices into service in the Union. It covers evidence and conformity before market entry, responsibilities in the supply chain, traceability, and monitoring after devices reach the market.

The framework is built around two core acts: the MDR for medical devices and the IVDR for in-vitro diagnostic devices. Patients, users and healthcare professionals are affected by the safety and performance system, while economic operators such as manufacturers, authorised representatives, importers and distributors carry specific regulatory responsibilities.

Official overview: Commission medical-devices framework.

Products: MDR and IVDR

Start with the product and its intended purpose. These branches orient the question; they are not a personal legal determination.

MDR

Medical Devices Regulation

Regulation (EU) 2017/745

Start here for medical devices for human use and accessories. The MDR also reaches specified Annex XVI product groups without an intended medical purpose when the Regulation's conditions apply.

Boundary: the MDR does not cover IVDs governed by the IVDR, and it contains other product-boundary and exclusion rules that may matter.

Official text: MDR, Regulation (EU) 2017/745 on EUR-Lex. Background: EUR-Lex MDR summary.

IVDR

In Vitro Diagnostic Medical Devices Regulation

Regulation (EU) 2017/746

Start here for in-vitro diagnostic medical devices for human use, their accessories and performance studies. The definition depends on the manufacturer's intended purpose for examining human specimens and providing diagnostic or related information.

Important laboratory boundary: general laboratory or research-use products are outside the IVDR unless, considering their characteristics, the manufacturer specifically intends them for in-vitro diagnostic examination.

Official text: IVDR, Regulation (EU) 2017/746 on EUR-Lex. Background: EUR-Lex IVDR summary (last updated 24 August 2022).

Roles and duties

You do not have to be the manufacturer to need these rules. Responsibilities differ by role and device; these are orientation points, not a substitute for the relevant provisions.

Do not infer exclusion from this list. Other roles and special settings can matter, roles can overlap, and duties depend on the product and activity. This page routes the legal questions; it does not decide an individual compliance position.

Role definitions: Commission economic-operator guidance.

Manufacturer

Designs/manufactures or has the device made and markets it under its name; carries the primary conformity system.

Responsibilities: Ensure devices are designed and manufactured to the applicable requirements, maintain risk/evaluation systems and technical documentation, and support post-market monitoring.

Official text: MDR Article 10 — open EUR-Lex act text; IVDR Article 10 — open EUR-Lex act text.

Authorised representative

Acts in the Union under a written mandate for specified tasks for a manufacturer established outside the Union.

Responsibilities: The written mandate specifies the tasks; this overview does not enumerate them.

Role guidance: Commission guidance for authorised representatives.

Importer

Places a device from a third country on the Union market.

Responsibilities: Carries its own checks before placing the device on the market, and has traceability, cooperation and corrective responsibilities.

Role guidance: Commission guidance for importers.

Distributor

Makes devices available in the supply chain.

Responsibilities: Must act with due care and perform specified checks before making devices available, with duties around complaints, non-conformity and cooperation.

Role guidance: Commission guidance for distributors.

Other affected groups

Patients, users, healthcare professionals, health institutions, notified bodies and authorities can all be affected by the framework, but their position is not the same as an economic operator's duties.

Context: Commission medical-devices framework overview.

Dates and current operation

26 May 2021

MDR general application / predecessor replacement

Regulation 2017/745 replaced Directives 90/385/EEC and 93/42/EEC from this date, subject to transition rules.

26 May 2022

IVDR general application / predecessor replacement

Regulation 2017/746 replaced Directive 98/79/EC from this date, subject to transition rules.

These dates do not settle every legacy-device case. Later EU measures preserve or extend transition routes for specified devices subject to conditions. The relevant product, certificate/declaration, risk class and timing can change the answer.

Transition sources: Commission medical-devices transition overview; Commission IVDR transitional provisions.

Current operational milestone — 28 May 2026

The first four EUDAMED modules became mandatory to use from this date after Commission Decision (EU) 2025/2371 declared them functional and the six-month transition triggered by Regulation (EU) 2024/1860 ran. This does not mean all six EUDAMED modules are already mandatory.

Official sources: Commission EUDAMED overview; Decision (EU) 2025/2371.

Selected later changes and current procedure

This is an orientation set, not a complete list of post-2017 acts. The Commission's current overview also lists later implementing and delegated measures; deeper act-family coverage remains in the relationship browser.

Enacted developments

Regulation (EU) 2024/1860

Amends both MDR and IVDR around gradual EUDAMED roll-out, information obligations for interruption/discontinuation of supply and IVDR transitional provisions.

Official act: Regulation (EU) 2024/1860 on EUR-Lex.

2025–2026 measures continue beyond 2024/1860

Examples in the current official overview include Decision (EU) 2025/2371 on EUDAMED functionality, Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/977 on conformity-assessment/notified-body requirements, and Delegated Regulations (EU) 2026/1359 and 2026/1451 amending specific MDR exemption lists. This landing page does not enumerate the full act family.

Official overview: Commission medical-devices regulations overview.

Proposal — not enacted

COM(2025) 1023 is not current binding Union law. The Commission proposed a further targeted revision in December 2025. Procedure 2025/0404(COD) remained ongoing at this page's 28 September 2026 evidence checkpoint.

Procedure record: EUR-Lex procedure 2025/0404(COD).

Context

How the rules fit together

Once the current purpose and scope are clear, the relationship view helps explain where the two Regulations came from and what hangs around them.

  • Directive 90/385/EEC (active implantable devices) → MDR
  • Directive 93/42/EEC (medical devices) → MDR
  • Directive 98/79/EC (in-vitro diagnostics) → IVDR

Replacement does not imply every predecessor effect vanished on one date: transition rules preserve defined routes for some devices.
