# Discovery — an inspector's categorical finding changes legal state

**Date:** 2026-09-23  
**Issue:** #80  
**Status:** second orthogonal case confirms the direct finding pattern

## Why maritime inspection

The HPAI case could still have been a special feature of animal-health science.

Port State Control removes:

- laboratory diagnosis;
- epidemiology;
- geographic zones.

What remains is an authority making an evidenced professional determination
about a concrete subject under unchanged law.

## BAHAR

The Paris MoU records the general cargo ship **BAHAR**, IMO 8230156, as detained
in Psakhna, Greece, on 18 July 2026.

The notice states:

- 13 deficiencies were found;
- all 13 were grounds for detention.

Directive 2009/16/EC Article 19 requires the port-State competent authority to
ensure detention or stoppage where inspection reveals deficiencies clearly
hazardous to safety, health or the environment.

Annex X governs the inspector's professional detention judgment.

The exact deficiency count is evidence about the inspection.

It is **not** what makes this a metric-trigger case.

The legally important direct truth is categorical:

> the competent inspection process determined that the deficiencies were
> grounds for detention.

## Comparison with HPAI

HPAI:

```
authority confirms disease present
        ↓
unchanged disease-control law reacts
        ↓
separate spatial derivation creates zones
```

BAHAR:

```
authority determines detainable deficiencies
        ↓
unchanged port-state-control law reacts
        ↓
ship detained
```

The shared primitive is not “scientific finding.”

It is:

> **Authoritative Finding**

A direct, evidenced determination by a legally relevant authority or official
control system that a categorical condition holds for a concrete subject.

## What the direct finding should own

- finding identity;
- finding character;
- authority / official system;
- subject identity;
- finding date;
- categorical condition / proposition;
- direct source evidence;
- legal relevance to binding rule(s).

## What it should not own

- generic legal consequences;
- enforcement workflow;
- spatial geometry;
- temporal application periods;
- source textual mutation.

That separation matters because the same finding can feed different downstream
legal rules, and those rules can later change without rewriting historical
finding truth.

## Architectural conclusion

The two cases now justify a narrow direct
`AUTHORITATIVE_FINDING` contract.

They do **not** yet justify a generic finding-rule engine.

The HPAI case still separately leaves one unresolved architecture question:
how should a finding + location + binding radius rule produce canonical legal
geometry?

Only one proof case exists for that spatial layer, so it remains a pinned gap.
