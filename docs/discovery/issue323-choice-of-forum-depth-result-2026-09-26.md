# Issue #323 — CHOICE_OF_FORUM_STATE residual-value depth test

Date: 2026-09-26  
Disposition: **KEEP_CLASS**

## Question

Does `CHOICE_OF_FORUM_STATE` retain a pre-specified consequential regression
distinction beyond competent ordinary EU jurisdiction-agreement doctrine, or is it only a
Needle label for mature doctrine?

## Evidence boundary

This depth test uses only already-accepted evidence:

- the frozen class definition and its three accepted cases;
- #276's original class-admission result;
- #279's matrimonial-property composition case;
- #277's anti-merge audit;
- #282's revised incumbent/residual-value rule;
- #321's class-support independence audit.

No new legal case, source hunt or model run was added.

## Strongest ordinary incumbent

Competent EU jurisdiction-agreement analysis already supplies the substantive legal rule.

For a jurisdiction agreement, the ordinary analysis asks:

1. which jurisdiction regime/default connecting rule applies;
2. whether a jurisdiction agreement exists;
3. whether it satisfies the relevant formal/substantive validity conditions;
4. what disputes/relationship/courts its scope covers;
5. whether the selection is exclusive or non-exclusive;
6. whether protected-party rules or mandatory exclusive-jurisdiction rules prevent or
   constrain the choice;
7. only then, whether the selected court displaces the default forum.

For the accepted cases:

- Brussels I Recast Article 25 supplies the selected-forum mechanism plus protected-party /
  Article 24 limits;
- Succession Regulation Article 5 supplies a separate court-selection mechanism whose
  precondition depends on the deceased's Article 22 law choice;
- matrimonial-property Regulation 2016/1103 shows forum choice can coexist with separate
  participation and applicable-law state.

Needle claims no doctrinal novelty over that analysis.

## Exact Needle regression distinction

The class's residual state is narrower than the doctrine:

> **selected-court state must be preserved and tested independently from both default
> jurisdiction connectors and governing-law choice.**

That state carries its own evidence and pass/fail consequences:

- evidence requirement: existence, actors, validity, scope, exclusivity and mandatory
  limits of the jurisdiction agreement;
- expected-answer consequence: the competent court can differ from the court produced by
  default domicile/connecting rules;
- anti-collapse consequence: a valid choice of governing law does not itself establish a
  choice of court, and vice versa;
- composition consequence: participation, applicable law and forum can all need separate
  resolution in one problem.

A regression that stores only forum defaults or only a governing-law choice can therefore
return the wrong competent court while every recorded law-choice fact remains correct.

That is a pre-specified consequential regression distinction, not descriptive nuance.

## Alternative 1 — ordinary jurisdiction doctrine only

### What survives

At the level of an individual legal problem, competent ordinary doctrine fully predicts:

- the evidence that must be checked;
- the selected/default forum relationship;
- the relevant limits;
- the legally correct answer.

Therefore the class is **not** justified because doctrine is missing or because Needle has
a better legal rule.

### What is lost

If the corpus removes the class while retaining only prose case records, it loses the
explicit reusable grouping:

> these exposed failures all test whether selected-forum state is being omitted,
> conflated with default jurisdiction, or inferred from a different private-choice state.

The individual cases remain understandable, but a regression consumer can no longer select
or compare this failure family as a stable corpus dimension without reconstructing that
relation from titles/decisive-trap prose.

Under the post-#282 rule, that organisational/test role can count only when the label owns
a consequential state. Here it does: the expected court and required evidence change.

This is therefore **not lossless at the corpus-regression level**, even though the
underlying doctrine is complete.

## Alternative 2 — merge into CHOICE_OF_LAW_STATE + generic private-choice metadata

**Reject.**

The accepted Succession and matrimonial-property evidence makes the loss concrete.

Choice of law and choice of forum can:

- be exercised by different actors;
- have different validity conditions;
- have different legal effects;
- coexist in the same instrument/problem;
- point to different States;
- fail independently.

A merged "private choice" state would permit the exact consequential error the corpus is
meant to prevent:

> a system can possess the governing-law choice and still select the wrong court.

This alternative therefore loses an expected-answer distinction and an evidence
requirement. It fails #282's lossless test.

## Alternative 3 — retire class, keep cases as unclassified reference examples

This is the strongest retirement option.

It preserves:

- all three case records;
- their decisive-trap prose;
- their evidence owners;
- their exposure/regression-only status.

But it loses the explicit class-level regression owner.

The current corpus contract also requires every case to carry at least one class. That
technical fact is **not** scientific evidence for keeping the class, but it makes the
retirement cost visible: implementing "keep cases, no class" would require either a new
unclassified/reference case mode or a misleading reassignment.

Such schema cost would not save an otherwise redundant class. The class survives because
the reusable selected-forum state already meets the residual-value test; the validator
constraint is secondary.

## Why this is not class-count preservation

The KEEP result does not rely on:

- the class already existing;
- three accepted cases;
- private international law being important;
- mature doctrine being complex;
- symmetry with `CHOICE_OF_LAW_STATE`;
- the inconvenience of changing the frozen corpus.

The positive reason is one regression distinction:

> **court-choice state changes the competent-court answer and requires evidence that
> cannot be inferred from governing-law choice or default forum connectors.**

The class materially organises and tests exactly that distinction across three accepted
cases.

## Boundary remains narrow

KEEP does not broaden the class.

It continues to require:

- a legally relevant jurisdiction agreement / court-selection mechanism;
- case-specific validity/scope/exclusivity analysis;
- preservation of protected-party and mandatory exclusive-jurisdiction limits.

It does not own:

- every jurisdiction question;
- default jurisdiction generally;
- applicable-law choice;
- Member-State participation;
- territorial scope;
- recognition/passport activation.
