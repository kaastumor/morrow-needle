## Claim under test

> For a lean manufacturer/compliance user who already knows which standard/regime matters, the
> Candidate-B status card makes the operative OJ-reference state, legal consequence and timing
> faster/easier to determine without reducing answer quality versus the free official-source
> workflow.

This is a usability/convenience claim.

It is **not**:

- a product-capability novelty claim;
- a full conformity-assessment claim;
- a comparison with paid competitors;
- a willingness-to-pay test.

## Target user

A person who regularly handles product compliance but does not need to be a standards-law specialist,
for example:

- compliance/regulatory lead in a small manufacturer;
- product/quality manager responsible for CE documentation;
- engineer with regulatory responsibility;
- specialist adviser serving small manufacturers.

## Frozen task set

Use the same five #411 cases.

For each case ask the participant, as of the specified query date:

1. Is the OJ reference cited, cited with restriction, or not cited?
2. What presumption-of-conformity consequence follows?
3. What restriction/scope matters?
4. What legal event owns the state?
5. Is a future transition already fixed?
6. What should **not** be inferred from the status?

Do not add easier/harder cases after seeing performance.

## Arm O — free official sources

Provide:

- relevant Commission harmonised-standards/formal-objection navigation;
- relevant EUR-Lex/OJ decision;
- ordinary browser/search;
- participant may take notes.

Do not deliberately obscure source links.

## Arm B — Candidate-B card

Provide the disposable static MVP.

The participant may open every underlying official evidence link.

## Design

Preferred:

- within-participant crossover;
- split five cases into two mechanically fixed sets plus one calibration case;
- counterbalance arm order across participants where possible;
- do not show the answer from the other arm before completion.

For a single early usability session, do not pretend this is population science.

## Measures

Required:

- final status correctness;
- legal-effect/presumption correctness;
- restriction/future-transition correctness;
- time-to-confident-answer;
- number of official-source openings/reopenings;
- participant confidence;
- observed confusion / correction events.

After both arms ask:

- Which workflow would you choose for the next similar task?
- What information was missing?
- What felt redundant?
- What would make the card unsafe to rely on?
- Would you want this only for known standards, or also for a product portfolio?

## Success threshold

Before the session, define a simple threshold.

Suggested first usability gate:

- no reduction in substantive correctness;
- fewer source-navigation steps on at least the legally non-trivial cases;
- participant prefers the card for at least one recurring workflow;
- no evidence that the compact rendering creates false certainty.

Do not require a dramatic time ratio from a one-person pilot.

## Kill / revise rules

Revise or stop the wedge if:

- users still need to reconstruct the same official chain to trust the card;
- the compact status hides a material restriction or non-implication;
- source navigation is already trivial in the free official workflow;
- users primarily need technical-standard content/testing rather than status;
- the card creates dangerous confidence outside its evidence window.

If the card is useful:

> next compare it against an actually accessed standards/compliance product, per #411.

No product platform is earned from a usability win.
