# Issue #411 — Candidate B standards-status product capability challenge

Date: 2026-09-27  
Mode: **VALIDATE — CANDIDATE B / STANDARDS-STATUS PRODUCT CAPABILITY**

## Result

# **DIRECT_PRODUCT_ACCESS_REQUIRED_BEFORE_COMPARISON**

The public market is materially closer to Candidate B than an "empty market" framing would imply.

Publicly documented products already offer combinations of:

- harmonised-standard monitoring;
- product/profile relevance;
- OJ citation tracking;
- standard revision/withdrawal alerts;
- future transition dates;
- historical version/change records;
- source/evidence links;
- product-compliance impact workflows.

Therefore Candidate B has **no earned novelty claim**.

However, public documentation is not sufficient to determine whether any accessible product
actually answers the frozen five-case legal-status questions with the same decision-ready
current/historical/future state as the Candidate-B card.

Public silence on formal-objection, restriction and as-of semantics remains **UNKNOWN**, not
evidence of absence.

The next discriminating capability evidence requires direct product access or a public sample
that exposes equivalent fields.

## 2. Official/free baseline

The strongest free baseline is already substantial.

Commission harmonised-standards pages provide:

- current consolidated reference lists;
- formal-objection decisions;
- RSS feeds;
- regime-specific publication pages.

EUR-Lex/OJ provides the binding decisions, legal dates and text.

The Commission formal-objection page explicitly distinguishes decisions that:

- publish / enable publication;
- do not publish;
- publish with restrictions;
- maintain;
- maintain with restrictions;
- withdraw references.

This means Candidate B gets **zero credit** for merely finding the relevant decision.

The possible value is only in turning fragmented official evidence into a maintained,
date-sensitive, decision-ready state.

| Official owning evidence | **YES** | Source evidence often claimed; exact decision linkage unclear |
| Non-implication guardrails | **YES** | Publicly unclear |
| General monitoring / alerts | **NOT A DIFFERENTIATOR** | Fully occupied |

The remaining possible residual is therefore **not monitoring**.

It is the combination:

> **legal-effect state + exact owning event + temporal history/future + unsafe-inference boundary**

for a specific standard/regime.

Whether competitors already provide that combination is not answerable from public marketing/help
material alone.

## 5. Needle control

The existing Candidate-B core can represent:

- ordinary cited state;
- cited-with-restriction state;
- formal non-publication;
- scheduled future withdrawal;
- post-withdrawal state;
- historical before/after queries;
- unchanged top-level NOT_CITED state with a different legal/evidentiary reason.

It uses the already-frozen:

> `authoritative-dynamic-set-v0.1`

No new ontology was required.

The control therefore passes the internal representation gate.

That does **not** prove product value.

---

## 6. Strongest red team

The card may simply be a more explicit rendering of information a competent user can already
obtain from:

- Commission summary lists;
- formal-objection page;
- EUR-Lex;
- standards-body or commercial alerts.

If so, its only advantage may be convenience.

Convenience can be product value, especially for lean teams, but it must be measured as such.

Do not relabel convenience as scientific distinctiveness.

A second risk is that the most compelling competitors already appear to offer the same
product-level future/status workflow, and their private product views may be substantially richer.

---

## 7. Why Candidate B remains alive

The capability challenge does **not** produce `NO_MEASURABLE_RESIDUAL`.

It produces an evidence boundary:

> public contracts are close enough that claiming a capability gap would be dishonest, but not
> detailed enough to establish full equivalence on the frozen legal-status workload.

The job itself is independently real:

- official EU legal effect changes with OJ citation/restriction/withdrawal state;
- multiple commercial products already productise standards monitoring;
- BSI explicitly targets SMEs in an adjacent vertical;
- Grecta and other products market exactly the pain of product-specific standard transitions.

This is enough to preserve Candidate B as a product-value hypothesis.
