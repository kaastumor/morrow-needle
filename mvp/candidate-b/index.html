Low Voltage Directive

Directive 2014/35/EU · recast · EEA relevance

In force
Evidence checked 27 September 2026. EUR-Lex currently exposes a consolidated text dated 30 May 2026. This page is an evidence-bounded view, not live legal monitoring.

At a glance

What this act is and where it sits

Directive 2014/35/EU is the current EU Low Voltage Directive for electrical equipment designed for use within specified voltage limits. It recast Directive 2006/95/EC, which had codified the earlier Council Directive 73/23/EEC. The act is based on Article 114 TFEU, sits within the EU product-law framework, and uses harmonised standards as one route to a presumption of conformity.

The Directive entered into force on 18 April 2014. Member States were to apply the required national measures from 20 April 2016, the same date on which Directive 2006/95/EC was repealed. The current consolidated text also incorporates Directive (EU) 2024/2749, which added internal-market-emergency procedures applying from 30 May 2026.

Operative dependency

What flows from Article 12

Article 12 provides that electrical equipment conforming to harmonised standards, or parts of standards, whose references have been published in the Official Journal is presumed to conform to the safety objectives covered by those standards or parts.

  1. Directive 2014/35/EU · Article 12 Creates the presumption-of-conformity mechanism.
  2. Harmonised-standard reference in the OJ The reference state matters: cited, restricted or not cited.
  3. Standard-specific legal effect The presumption applies only within the scope actually covered by the published reference.
