# #221 result — private-origin normative artifacts with public legal effect

Date: 2026-09-24  
Issue: #221

## Decision

# **REVISE_EXISTING_CLASS**

Keep the class name:

`PRIVATE_ORIGIN_LEGAL_RECOGNITION`

Broaden its definition from private-origin **determinations** to private-origin
outputs/artifacts generally:

> A private-origin output, technical artifact or determination can have a bounded
> legal role because public law recognises, approves, incorporates or adopts it,
> without converting the private source into a public authority.

## EASA/Airbus positive

EASA AD 2026-0155 identifies Airbus A380 ALS Part 2 Revision 12 and accepts later
approved revisions.

EASA separately explains that ALS content is part of the certificated type
design, contains mandatory maintenance/limitations, is approved by EASA and is
made Mandatory Continued Airworthiness Information through AD action when new or
more restrictive.

Sources:
- https://ad.easa.europa.eu/ad/2026-0155
- https://www.easa.europa.eu/en/the-agency/faqs/airworthiness-directives-ads

## Harmonised-standard positive

CJEU Case C-588/21 P records that harmonised standards are developed by private
bodies but acquire legal effects through the EU standardisation/OJ-publication
chain; the Court held the requested harmonised standards form part of EU law.

Source:
https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:62021CJ0588

## Negative control

EASA states that a manufacturer's Service Bulletin labelled “mandatory” is not
legally mandatory merely because the private issuer uses that label. Where an AD
covers the SB, the public AD supplies the mandatory chain.

Source:
https://www.easa.europa.eu/en/faq/19494

The Airbus ALS chain remains supporting generality evidence rather than a new
