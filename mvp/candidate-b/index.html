At a glance

What this act is and where it sits

Directive 2014/35/EU sets EU safety and market rules for electrical equipment within specified voltage ranges. Article 1 combines a high level of protection for people, domestic animals and property with the functioning of the internal market. It recast Directive 2006/95/EC, which had codified the earlier Council Directive 73/23/EEC.

The Directive entered into force on 18 April 2014. Member States were to apply the required national measures from 20 April 2016, the same date on which Directive 2006/95/EC was repealed. The current consolidated text also incorporates Directive (EU) 2024/2749, which added internal-market-emergency procedures applying from 30 May 2026.

Applicability

Who / what does this concern?

“Who this applies to” is not one legal question. This view separates the Directive's formal addressee from the products, market activities and economic-operator roles that the harmonised national rules concern.

Formal addressee
EU Member States. Article 29 addresses the Directive to the Member States; as a directive, it operates through national implementation rather than by treating this label as a personalised conclusion for an individual business.
Object / material scope
Electrical equipment designed for 50–1,000 V AC or 75–1,500 V DC, subject to Annex II exclusions.
Market activity
Article 2 defines making available on the market around supply for distribution, consumption or use on the Union market in the course of a commercial activity, whether paid or free of charge.
Economic operators
Manufacturer, authorised representative, importer and distributor are distinct roles. The Directive assigns different responsibilities to those roles.
Territory / market context
The operative market concepts are framed around the Union market; the act is also marked as text with EEA relevance. This page does not determine a reader's national implementation position.
Time represented here
This view describes the EU-level legal picture as of 27 September 2026, including the 2024 amendment that applies from 30 May 2026.
Major Annex II exclusions

Article 1 excludes the equipment and phenomena listed in Annex II. The represented list includes:

  • electrical equipment for use in an explosive atmosphere;
  • electrical equipment for radiology and medical purposes;
  • electrical parts for goods and passenger lifts;
  • electricity meters;
  • plugs and socket outlets for domestic use;
  • electric fence controllers;
  • radio-electrical interference;
  • certain specialised electrical equipment for ships, aircraft or railways that complies with relevant international safety provisions;
  • custom-built evaluation kits for professionals used solely at research and development facilities.

Important: being outside LVD scope does not by itself tell you which other EU or national legal regime applies.

Operative dependency

What flows from Article 12

Article 12 provides that electrical equipment conforming to harmonised standards, or parts of standards, whose references have been published in the Official Journal is presumed to conform to the safety objectives covered by those standards or parts. That presumption is limited to the objectives actually covered; it is not a general certificate of full product compliance.

Other represented routes: where Article 12 harmonised standards have not been drawn up and published, Article 13 provides a route involving published IEC safety provisions. Where neither Article 12 harmonised standards nor Article 13 international standards are available in the stated way, Article 14 provides a further national-standard route subject to its conditions.

  1. Directive 2014/35/EU · Article 12 Creates the presumption-of-conformity mechanism.
  2. Harmonised-standard reference in the OJ The reference state matters: cited, restricted or not cited.
  3. Standard-specific legal effect The presumption applies only within the scope actually covered by the published reference.
