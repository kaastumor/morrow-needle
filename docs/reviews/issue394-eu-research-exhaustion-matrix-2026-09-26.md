## Result

# **EU_PUBLIC_RESEARCH_NOT_EXHAUSTED**

The sponsor's instruction changes the interpretation of the current frontier.

Issue #377 legitimately parked **EU legal-state prevalence sampling**.

It did **not** exhaust EU-specific research as a whole.

Most obvious case families, taxonomy-growth paths and public prevalence sampling are now at
their evidence boundary. But one material public question remains untested:

> **Can the official EU legal-information stack itself represent the independently moving
> operative states Needle found, or do those states live outside the legislation/publication
> metadata contract?**

This question is public, falsifiable and materially different from:

- finding another hard EU-law case;
- another prevalence sample;
- another commercial-product marketing scan;
- another benchmark defect hunt.

If the official stack already represents the relevant state losslessly, that would reduce
Needle's conceptual distinctiveness.

If it does not, that would clarify precisely where the official information contract ends
without establishing commercial value.

## 4. Why official EU information-model sufficiency is the remaining public lane

This was not actually tested by #377.

#377 asked:

> what authoritative sources/states are needed to answer a legal question?

The remaining question asks:

> **what does the EU's official legal-information data model claim to represent, and can it
> encode the decisive state without a bespoke parallel ontology?**

Those are different questions.

### Official baseline is stronger than Needle should assume

Current Publications Office material establishes that:

- **ELI v1.5** is the current European Legislation Identifier ontology and defines a common
  data model for exchanging legislation metadata;
- **Cellar** is the Publications Office semantic repository underlying services including
  EUR-Lex and exposes metadata through linked-data/SPARQL and REST interfaces;
- Cellar uses the **Common Data Model (CDM)** and a Work/Expression/Manifestation-style
  publication model;
- **ELI-Impact (ELI-I)** extends core ELI to describe legislative impacts, text modifications
  and updates to consolidated versions, including impacts from sources such as court
  decisions;
- ELI includes synchronisation machinery for metadata exchange;
- EUR-Lex exposes national transposition measures, while expressly warning that notified
  national information is supplied under Member-State responsibility and does not prejudge
  Commission verification.

Therefore any Needle claim that "official EU legal information is just documents and text"
would be false.

The official stack already has serious semantic structure.

### What is not yet established

The public descriptions alone do not tell us whether the following are representable as
first-class, effective-time-aware states or only recoverable by external legal reasoning:

1. **CBAM-type dynamic authoritative input**
   - a period-specific official numeric value changes an unchanged legal formula;

2. **CER-type entity identification**
   - a Member State identifies a concrete entity and legal consequences attach;

3. **CSRD-type permitted national option**
   - EU law authorises bounded Member-State divergence for a cohort/time window;

4. **MDR/IVDR transition eligibility**
   - current legal treatment depends on historical class/certificate/application/notified-
     body facts;

5. **CPR product-family migration**
   - old/new legal regimes switch for different product families as harmonised technical
     specifications reach their legal boundary;

6. **OJ citation / non-citation of private standards**
   - a private technical standard exists independently while EU legal recognition changes;

7. **administrative authorisation history**
   - a separately created public-law decision (for example a marketing authorisation) is an
     operative input to another EU rule.

These are precisely the seven strong residual structures left after #377's anti-inflation
review.

## 5. Bounded successor question

Exactly one public successor is earned:

> **OFFICIAL EU LEGAL-INFORMATION REPRESENTATION SUFFICIENCY**

Question:

> Can the current official EU information stack — ELI v1.5, ELI-Impact, Cellar/CDM,
> EUR-Lex metadata and national-transposition metadata — represent each of the seven #377
> residual state structures losslessly enough to reconstruct the operative legal state, or
> does the decisive state necessarily live in an external owner/registry/inference layer?

### Required output per residual

Classify exactly one:

- `OFFICIAL_MODEL_DIRECT`
  - the relevant state/effective boundary is explicitly representable in the official model;

- `OFFICIAL_MODEL_LINKED_OWNER`
  - official legislation metadata can link to the necessary legal/resource owner but the
    decisive state lives in another authoritative system;

- `LEGAL_INFERENCE_REQUIRED`
  - the source facts are represented but the operative state requires legal reasoning not
    encoded as metadata;

- `MODEL_GAP_CONFIRMED`
  - official specification lacks a lossless way to represent the required state and no
    official linked-owner contract closes it;

- `INDETERMINATE`
  - public official specifications are insufficient to decide.

### Falsifier

A strong result against Needle would be:

> all or nearly all seven residuals are already explicitly represented or cleanly linked by
> the official EU data contract, making a separate Needle-style state model largely
> redundant at the information-model level.

That result must be welcomed.

### Guardrails

- inspect specifications, not just EUR-Lex UI;
- no new Needle schema;
- no implementation;
- no class/corpus growth;
- do not treat legal inference as metadata failure automatically;
- do not demand that ELI represent domain registries it never claims to own;
- distinguish **identifier/linkage sufficiency** from **operative-state sufficiency**;
- no commercial claim.
