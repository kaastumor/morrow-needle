# Navigator formative comparison — frozen tasks and facilitator answer key

**Facilitator-only. Do not give the answer key to participants before completion.**

## Task set A

### A1 — addressee versus people practically concerned

> Who is Directive 2014/35/EU formally addressed to? Name at least two economic-operator roles that the Directive nevertheless concerns in practice. Briefly explain why those are not contradictory answers.

**Acceptable core answer**
- Formal addressee: EU Member States.
- Practical/economic-operator examples: manufacturer, authorised representative, importer, distributor.
- Distinction: a directive is addressed to Member States while the implementing legal regime assigns obligations around market actors/products.

**Critical false inference**
- “It only applies to Member States, therefore manufacturers/importers are not concerned.”

**Evidence**
- Directive 2014/35/EU Article 29
- Articles 2, 6–9

---

### A2 — material scope and exclusion

> A domestic plug or socket outlet is electrical equipment operating within the LVD voltage range. From the material in front of you, is being inside the voltage range enough to conclude that it falls within the Directive? Explain.

**Acceptable core answer**
- No.
- Annex II expressly excludes plugs and socket outlets for domestic use.
- More generally, matching the voltage range alone is not enough; exclusions/conditions still matter.
- Being excluded does not by itself tell you which other legal regime governs it.

**Critical false inference**
- “Yes, because the voltage is in range.”

**Evidence**
- Article 1
- Annex II

---

### A3 — force versus application and predecessor

> When did Directive 2014/35/EU enter into force, and when did the required national measures start applying? What happened to Directive 2006/95/EC on that later date?

**Acceptable core answer**
- Entered into force: 18 Apr 2014.
- Required national measures applied from: 20 Apr 2016.
- Directive 2006/95/EC was repealed from 20 Apr 2016.

**Critical false inference**
- Treating 26 Feb 2014, 29 Mar 2014, 18 Apr 2014 and 20 Apr 2016 as interchangeable.

**Evidence**
- Articles 26–28

---

### A4 — imported definition

> The LVD uses the term “harmonised standard”. Where does the represented definition come from, and why does that external provision matter to Article 12?

**Acceptable core answer**
- LVD Article 2(9) imports the definition from Regulation (EU) No 1025/2012 Article 2(1)(c).
- Article 12 operates on harmonised standards, so the imported definition supplies the meaning of a term needed by the mechanism.
- This does not mean the whole of Regulation 1025/2012 is imported into every LVD question.

**Critical false inference**
- “Because one definition is imported, Regulation 1025/2012 as a whole applies as if it were part of every LVD rule.”

**Evidence**
- LVD Article 2(9)
- Regulation 1025/2012 Article 2(1)(c)

## Task set B

### B1 — framework context versus ancestry

> Is Regulation (EC) No 765/2008 shown as a predecessor of Directive 2014/35/EU? What relationship is represented instead?

**Acceptable core answer**
- No.
- It is represented as product-law framework context, including accreditation, market surveillance, controls on products from third countries and general CE-marking principles.
- The displayed ancestry is 73/23/EEC → 2006/95/EC → 2014/35/EU.

**Critical false inference**
- Putting Regulation 765/2008 into the predecessor/recast lineage.

**Evidence**
- LVD recital 2
- lineage evidence in Directive 2006/95/EC and Directive 2014/35/EU

---

### B2 — presumption boundary

> If electrical equipment conforms to a harmonised standard whose reference is published in the Official Journal, what does Article 12 let you presume, and what does it not establish by itself?

**Acceptable core answer**
- Presumption of conformity with the Article 3 / Annex I safety objectives covered by that standard or part.
- It does not by itself establish complete product compliance with every applicable legal requirement.

**Critical false inference**
- “A cited harmonised standard proves the product is fully legally compliant.”

**Evidence**
- LVD Article 12

---

### B3 — external publication/objection procedure

> Which external provisions are represented as helping determine whether the OJ-reference state behind Article 12 exists, is restricted or is withdrawn?

**Acceptable core answer**
- Regulation (EU) No 1025/2012 Article 10(6) and Article 11.
- Article 10(6): publication of qualifying references.
- Article 11: formal-objection machinery / decisions concerning publication, restriction, maintenance or withdrawal in the represented explanation.

**Critical false inference**
- Treating this as merely a generic cross-reference with no operative role.

**Evidence**
- Regulation 1025/2012 Articles 10(6) and 11
- LVD Article 12 context

---

### B4 — currentness and date semantics

> The page/note shows 27 September 2026 and 30 May 2026. What different things do those dates represent? Does 30 May 2026 mean the original LVD first came into force then?

**Acceptable core answer**
- 27 Sep 2026: legal view / evidence verification date in this frozen representation.
- 30 May 2026: current consolidated-text/source-version date and, separately, the application date represented for the 2024 amendment.
- No: original LVD entered into force on 18 Apr 2014.
- The fact that two different legal/source concepts share 30 May 2026 does not make them the same concept.

**Critical false inference**
- “The LVD came into force on 30 May 2026.”
- “Last checked”, consolidation date and legal-application date are interchangeable.

**Evidence**
- Articles 26–28
- Directive (EU) 2024/2749 Article 11
- EUR-Lex consolidated-text metadata

## Confidence question after every task

Ask:

> How confident are you in that answer: low, medium or high?

Do not tell the participant whether the answer was correct until the task block ends.

## Source-verification probe

For one answer in each representation, ask:

> If you had to rely on this in real work or study, where would you click/open next to verify it?

Record whether the participant can identify an appropriate official source without prompting.

## Debrief after both representations

Ask, in this order:

1. Which representation would you start with next time you already knew the EU act you were looking at? Why?
2. Which made it easier to understand how different laws/provisions connect?
3. Which made it easier to distinguish dates with different legal meanings?
4. Which made it easier to find the source for a claim?
5. Was anything on the Navigator misleading, too confident or visually confusing?
6. Was anything in the note easier than the Navigator?
7. At what point would you leave either representation and open EUR-Lex?
8. What, if anything, did you expect to find but could not?

Do not ask “Did you like it?” as the primary value question.
