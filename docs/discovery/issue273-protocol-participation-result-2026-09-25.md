# #273 result — Protocol 21/22 generality for differentiated Member-State participation

Date: 2026-09-25  
Issue: #273  
Depth lane: run 1/3 under the #282 falsifier

## Decision

# **REVISE_EXISTING_CLASS**

Retain `DIFFERENTIATED_MEMBER_STATE_PARTICIPATION` as the owner, add one Protocol 21
derivation case, and sharpen the boundary so that separate agreement-based application is
not mistaken for participation in the EU act as Union law.

Corpus consequence:

> **80 cases / 26 classes**

No new class.

## Frozen question

Does the unchanged participation-class definition identify the same decisive evidence
owner under Protocol 21/22 as under enhanced cooperation, such that one regression
expectation can safely cover both mechanisms?

The answer is:

> **Yes for Ireland's Protocol 21 post-adoption opt-in; not as a generic binary reading of
> Treaty-protocol status.**

The positive mechanism transfers unusually strongly because Protocol 21 Article 4
expressly imports the Article 331(1) TFEU participation procedure *mutatis mutandis*.
But the Denmark side of Regulation (EU) 2024/1351 shows why the class needs an explicit
route boundary: Denmark is not bound by the Regulation as Union law under Protocol 22,
while separate EU-Denmark agreement machinery can make specified parts applicable.

## Pre-specified regression consequence

The consequential error is not merely calling Protocol 21 legally different from enhanced
cooperation.

The regression failure is:

> answer whether Regulation (EU) 2024/1351 bound/applied to Ireland at a relevant date
> from the Regulation's EU label or its adoption-state recital alone, without resolving
> Ireland's later participation act.

That produces a different legal answer across time.

A second error must be excluded rather than promoted into the same case:

> infer that Denmark is simply outside every operative rule in Regulation 2024/1351 from
> Protocol 22 non-participation alone, ignoring the separate agreement-based route for
> Parts III, V and VII.

That latter route is not direct participation in the Regulation as Union law.

## Primary evidence

### 1. Adoption state — Ireland outside

Regulation (EU) 2024/1351 recital 91 states that, under Protocol 21 Articles 1 and 2 and
Article 4a(1), Ireland did not take part in adoption and was not bound by or subject to the
Regulation, expressly **without prejudice to Article 4** of Protocol 21.

Source:
- https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1351/oj/eng

### 2. Protocol 21 post-adoption route

Protocol 21 Article 4 allows Ireland, after adoption of a Title V measure, to notify the
Council and Commission that it wishes to accept the measure. It then states that the
procedure in Article 331(1) TFEU applies *mutatis mutandis*.

Source:
- https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:12016E/PRO/21

This is decisive for the generality question: the opt-in mechanism does not merely resemble
later accession to enhanced cooperation. The Treaty deliberately reuses its participation
procedure.

### 3. Ireland actually moved state

Commission Decision (EU) 2024/2088 records Ireland's 27 June 2024 notification, confirms
Ireland's participation in Regulation 2024/1351 in Article 1, and entered into force on
22 August 2024.

Source:
- https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/en/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32024D2088

Therefore the relevant representation state is time-varying:

- at adoption / initial entry into force: Ireland was not bound under the Regulation's
  Protocol 21 state;
- after the Article 4 route and confirmation Decision: Ireland participates.

A historical answer cannot safely back-project the current participant state, while a
current answer cannot freeze the adoption-state recital.

### 4. Enhanced-cooperation comparison

Article 331(1) TFEU requires a Member State wishing to join enhanced cooperation in
progress to notify the Council and Commission and provides for Commission confirmation of
participation.

Source:
- https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:12016E331

Commission Decision (EU) 2024/807 supplies the concrete EPPO comparison for Poland:
notification under the enhanced-cooperation framework followed by Commission confirmation
of participation.

Source:
- https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32024D0807

The decisive evidence owner therefore transfers:

> **participation regime + Member State + relevant time + legally operative
> notification/confirmation state**

The underlying doctrines remain different. The regression owner does not require them to
be doctrinally identical.

## Denmark is a boundary, not a second validation

Regulation 2024/1351 recital 90 states that Denmark did not take part in adoption and is
not bound by or subject to the Regulation under Protocol 22.

The same recital immediately adds that Parts III, V and VII constitute amendments for the
separate EU-Denmark Dublin/Eurodac agreement and require Denmark to notify whether it will
implement them.

Source:
- https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1351/oj/eng

EUR-Lex's current legislative summary records that Denmark participates in Parts III, V
and VII.

Source:
- https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/LSU/?uri=CELEX:32024R1351

This prevents a simplistic negative case.

The correct boundary is:

> **non-participation in an EU act as Union law is not equivalent to absence of every
> linked legal route that may reproduce or implement part of that act's content.**

A separate agreement/incorporation mechanism must be resolved on its own terms. It does
not turn Denmark into a direct Protocol-22 participant in Regulation 2024/1351.

This is why Denmark is retained as boundary evidence only, not counted as an independent
positive or negative validation.

## Nearest-class refusal

### `MEMBER_STATE_PERMITTED_DIVERGENCE`

Not the same owner.

That class assumes the Union framework applies and asks which bounded national option,
parameter or more protective rule a Member State has exercised.

Here the first question is earlier:

> **is this Member State bound by / participating in this Union measure at the relevant
> time?**

Ireland's Article 4 opt-in changes that participation state; it is not a national
parameter inside an already-applicable framework.

### Separate agreement / incorporation route

Denmark's partial agreement-based application also demonstrates why the participation
class must not absorb every route by which equivalent Union-derived content becomes
operative.

The participation class owns direct Member-State participation in the act under the
relevant Union participation regime.

A separate agreement or incorporation route remains a separate evidence problem even
where its practical content overlaps.

## Class repair

Previous definition:

> An EU act can bind or apply only in a legally defined subset of Member States because
> of enhanced cooperation, opt-ins or Treaty protocols, and that participation set can
> change over time; applicability must be resolved against the act's participation regime
> and relevant date rather than inferred from the EU act label or today's participant
> list.

Revised definition:

> An EU act can bind or apply as Union law only in a legally defined subset of Member
> States because of enhanced cooperation, opt-ins or Treaty protocols, and that
> participation set can change over time; applicability must be resolved against the
> act's participation regime and relevant date rather than inferred from the EU act label
> or today's participant list. A separate agreement or incorporation route that makes
> equivalent or partial content operative does not by itself make a non-participating
> Member State a participant in the EU act.

This is a boundary clarification, not a new causal class.

## Added derivation case

### Ireland — Regulation (EU) 2024/1351 Protocol 21 post-adoption opt-in

Decisive trap:

> Regulation 2024/1351 recorded Ireland as non-participating and not bound at adoption,
> but Protocol 21 Article 4 allowed a later opt-in and Commission Decision 2024/2088
> confirmed participation. Applicability must therefore resolve Ireland's participation
> state at the relevant date rather than freeze either the adoption-state recital or the
> current participant list.

This supplies the missing dedicated Protocol/opt-in mechanism.

## Why this is not confirmation-only depth

#273 does add a positive case, but its information gain is not the extra example.

The run produced a sharper exclusion boundary:

- Protocol 21 opt-in genuinely transfers to the existing owner because Article 4 directly
  reuses Article 331(1);
- Protocol 22 non-participation cannot safely be represented as a simple negative bit
  where a separate agreement can make parts operative;
- the class is therefore tightened to participation **in the EU act as Union law**, while
  separate agreement/incorporation routes remain distinct.

This counts as a boundary improvement under the #282 depth falsifier.

Depth score:

> run **1/3: informative** — definition/boundary changed.

The three-run direction-review trigger is not close to firing.

## Limits

This result does **not** prove that every Treaty protocol fits this class.

It proves one Protocol 21 post-adoption opt-in mechanism using a Treaty path that
expressly reuses Article 331(1).

Future protocol claims still require their own evidence owner and route analysis.
